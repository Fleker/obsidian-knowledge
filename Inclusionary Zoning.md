* [The tweets criticize inclusionary zoning policies, arguing they unfairly burden middle-class renters with the cost of affordable housing while wealthy homeowners receive tax breaks.](https://x.com/maxdubler/status/1985118363199426807)
* [Matthew Yglesias argues that inclusionary zoning, a policy requiring developers to include affordable housing in new constructions, effectively acts as a tax on new housing, as evidenced by Kansas City's ordinance failing to produce affordable units.](https://x.com/mattyglesias/status/1990170451465982223)
* [The post criticizes Pittsburgh's Inclusionary Zoning (IZ) policy, arguing it doesn't address affordability or expand affordable units, and suggests advocacy groups are diverting time from more pressing issues like public transit, while creating a perception of action.](https://x.com/davidvatz/status/1958736507830673684)
* [Oregon may ban unfunded affordable housing mandates due to data showing they negatively impact homebuilding and increase housing costs, a practice California also employs.](https://x.com/realsaadasad/status/2034307847903666395)
* [Chris Elmendorf highlights a new study showing that typical inclusionary zoning policies in California reduce housing production by 31% and cost market-rate renters $775,000 per below-market-rate unit.](https://x.com/CSElmendorf/status/2078565514637697190)
