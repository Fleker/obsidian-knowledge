In New York, 15K homes caused rents to [go up ~$138/year o $12/month](https://x.com/aarmlovi/status/1866248791315136906). That's about 0.68% of rental supply.

The city council of <span class="private-link" title="This page is private" style="color: #94a3b8; cursor: not-allowed; text-decoration: dashed underline;">New York City</span> introduced a bill to allow one-and-two family home rentals. It [was protested](https://www.thecity.nyc/2024/12/09/airbnb-ban-tenants-not-tourists-city-council/) by the Hotel and Games Trade Council.

> Councilmember Farah Louis’ bill, Intro. 1107, would allow for rentals of less than 30 days in one- and two-family houses, increasing the number of guests allowed to stay from two to four adults, plus their children. Under the bill, the primary occupants would not need to be present during the guests’ stay, so they could rent space while on vacation, but would still need to register with the city. The hosts may also install locks internally to restrict guests from certain rooms, closets or drawers.
>
> Airbnb, which has spent over a million dollars this year on local lobbying, [said](https://news.airbnb.com/nyc-rules-higher-prices-for-travelers-no-impact-on-housing/) the 2023 law didn’t do much to alleviate the housing crisis or expense of renting in New York City, while helping fuel a [spike in hotel prices](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/11/24/nyregion/new-york-city-hotels.html).
