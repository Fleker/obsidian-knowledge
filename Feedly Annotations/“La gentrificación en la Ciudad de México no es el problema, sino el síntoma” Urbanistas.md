Furthermore, it raises the need to diversify housing access models with schemes such as social income and housing cooperatives , which have proven effective in countries such as Uruguay, Spain, and South Korea.
    
Specialists agree that the Mexican real estate market is "monopolistic, opaque, and with significant information asymmetries." Therefore, they maintain that the construction of social housing must go hand in hand with fiscal, legal, and urban planning instruments that allow the State to intervene more effectively in the market.
    
Regarding Decree 1, the strategy presented by the city government to combat gentrification , Zentella Gómez acknowledged that “building more social housing is necessary, but insufficient if it is not accompanied by fiscal and legal instruments that regulate the market.”
    
In 2018, the capital produced an average of 20,000 new homes per year, compared to an estimated demand of 70,000 units. By 2025, the situation has become critical: the annual projection is for just 2,000 homes , with only 707 registered in the first half of the year.

According to the College, this production covers less than 1% of the annual need, creating an "unsustainable gap that fuels rising prices."
    
“The lack of sustained production of affordable housing and poor land management are the main drivers of rising residential prices and gentrification in Mexico City and its metropolitan area ,” the College study states.
