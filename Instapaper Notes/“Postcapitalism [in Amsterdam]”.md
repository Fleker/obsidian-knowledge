> - [ ] Romer pointed out in 1990 that information goods — if they can be copied and pasted infinitely, and used simultaneously without wear and tear — must fall in price under market conditions to a value close to zero. [↗](https://www.instapaper.com/read/811763710/4069292)

> But this new reliance on monopoly to maintain price means the essential market relationships are no longer organic. They have to be imposed each morning by lawyers and legislators. [↗](https://www.instapaper.com/read/811763710/4069294)

> This is just not happening fast enough. High value jobs are created — but not fast enough to replace the low value jobs. In fact, we’re creating a lot more low value jobs, just to keep people in work. [↗](https://www.instapaper.com/read/811763710/4069307)

> I hope everyone here understands the concept of externalities — because it is crucial. Networked business models create massive positive spillovers — network effects –where the data, or the wellbeing, or the utility created by network interactions is capturable and exploitable. [↗](https://www.instapaper.com/read/811763710/4069314)

> But it’s worth saying that geopolitifcal fragmentation is also possible. Its possible that we get a load of competitive currency moves that destroy globalisation. The globalisation process is already stalled and slowed down. Look at the failure of the Canadian/EU trade deal; look at Brexit; look at the stonewalling of TTIP. [↗](https://www.instapaper.com/read/811763710/4069319)

> Printing, gunpowder and the compass: these three have changed the whole face and state of things throughout the world [↗](https://www.instapaper.com/read/811763710/4069323)

> Instead of an economy consisting of the market and the state, you have an extra sector, which I call the non-market. It already exists: Linux, Wikipedia, Apache, all the open source tools and standards we use, all the existing knowledge downloadable for free; in the analog world — all the co-ops, the peer-to-peer lenders, the ethical banks and credit unions. [↗](https://www.instapaper.com/read/811763710/4069396)

> The left’s aim in the transition should be twofold: to push as much work as possible into the non-market sector. So that not only encyclopedias but some healthcare, education, wellness, cleaning up the beach — gets done in the free time created by mass automation. [↗](https://www.instapaper.com/read/811763710/4069401)

> precariat [↗](https://www.instapaper.com/read/811763710/4069413)

> At the same time the state — including city governments — needs to promote open source and non-profit business models. [↗](https://www.instapaper.com/read/811763710/4069415)

> A city has dense networks; they make change survivable; they also have enough power to resist the neoliberal elite when it tries to impose new, ever more bizarre, iterations of neoliberalism. [↗](https://www.instapaper.com/read/811763710/4069419)

> There’s other things to do; start a co-op; write an open-source App; work for free sometimes; create free stuff. That’s one part of the revolution. The important thing is: to demand the state ceases to support neoliberal solutions and actively supports collaborative business models. [↗](https://www.instapaper.com/read/811763710/4069421)
